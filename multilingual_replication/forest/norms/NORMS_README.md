# Concreteness norm files (standardised: `word`, `conc`, [`conc_sd`], ..., `source`, `scale`)

All files were taken from the ManyNorms / semanticprimeR GitHub release (SemanticPriming/semanticprimeR, v0.0.1),
which redistributes the published norms; the untouched originals are in `original/`. Duplicate word forms were
averaged. `conc` is the mean concreteness rating on the scale given in `scale` (correlations are scale-free).

| file | language | n | scale | reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hr_peti-stantic-2021.csv | Croatian | 5,961 | 1–5 | Peti-Stantić, A. et al. (2021). The Croatian psycholinguistic database: estimates for 6,000 nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs. Behav. Res. Methods 53, 1799–1816. Columns also give word class, hrWaC frequency and the noun's grammatical gender (`gram_gender`: m/f/n). |
| hr_coso-2019.csv | Croatian | 3,022 | 1–7 | Ćoso, B., Guasch, M., Ferré, P. & Hinojosa, J. A. (2019). Affective and concreteness norms for 3,022 Croatian words. Q. J. Exp. Psychol. 72, 2302–2312. |
| fa_nemati-2026_human-bestworst.csv | Persian | 2,924 | best–worst 0–1 | Nemati, F. et al. (2026). Extrapolated Persian Lexical Affect Norms (E-PLAN) from best–worst judgments of valence, arousal, dominance, and concreteness. Behav. Res. Methods (human-rated subset). |
| fa_nemati-2026_extrapolated.csv | Persian | 85,700 | best–worst 0–1 | Same source; machine-extrapolated scores — secondary check only. |
